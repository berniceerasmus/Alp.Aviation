Comparatives & Superlatives

✈ SOUTH AFRICA ⇄ TURKEY — Summer Holiday Comparison
GroupAlp Aviation
LevelA2+
Duration45 min
Students4–5
01

Welcome Back Warm-Up

5 min

Welcome the group back from summer break. Ask the class to mingle briefly (or answer round-robin if online) using these questions:

"Where did you go this summer?" · "Was it hot or cold?" · "Was it expensive or cheap?" · "How was the food?"

Write any adjectives students volunteer (hot, expensive, crowded, relaxing, far...) on the board — you'll recycle them in the grammar section.

Teacher note — Goal: surface adjectives naturally before naming the grammar. If the group is quiet, prompt with your own example first: "My summer was hot but relaxing — Turkey in August is really hot!"
02

Grammar Focus: Comparatives & Superlatives

12 min

Comparative — comparing two things

Use a comparative to compare two people, places, or things.

TR Karşılaştırma yaparken kullanılır — "daha" anlamı taşır.
Short adjectives (1 syllable)
adjective + -er + than
Turkey is hotter than South Africa in July.
Long adjectives (2+ syllables)
more + adjective + than
South Africa is more expensive than Turkey.

Superlative — comparing three or more

Use a superlative to say something is number one in a group.

TR Bir grup içinde "en" anlamı taşır.
Short adjectives
the + adjective + -est
The Mediterranean coast is the warmest region in Turkey.
Long adjectives
the most + adjective
Cape Town is one of the most beautiful cities in South Africa.

Spelling reminders

big → bigger (double the consonant)  ·  sunny → sunnier (y → i)  ·  safe → safer (just add -r)

Irregular forms

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
goodbetterthe best
badworsethe worst
farfarther / furtherthe farthest / furthest
Teacher note — Board these fast: elicit the pattern first ("What's the comparative of hot?"), only give the rule after they attempt it. Keep this stage under 12 minutes — the board activity is where it gets real practice.
03

Guided Practice: The Departures Board

10 min

Project or print the board below. It gives a general, typical-summer comparison of the two destinations — use it as the shared "data" for the sentences that follow.

✈ SUMMER HOLIDAY COMPARISON BOARD
CategorySouth AfricaTurkey
Flight time from Istanbul≈ 10–12 hrs—
Typical August weatherCool / winter seasonHot / dry summer
Popular activitySafari & wildlifeBeaches & sea
General cost levelHigher (long-haul)Lower (domestic/regional)
Crowds in AugustQuieterVery crowded
Note: general seasonal patterns — South Africa's winter falls during the Turkish summer.

Students complete the sentences using the board and the correct comparative or superlative form. Click Show to reveal an answer during class.

  • In August, Turkey is much ___ (hot) South Africa. hotter than
  • A trip to South Africa is usually ___ (expensive) a trip to Turkey. more expensive than
  • Turkish beaches are ___ (crowded) in August. the most crowded
  • South Africa is ___ (far) from Turkey than most European destinations. farther / further
  • For a wildlife safari, South Africa is ___ (good) choice than Turkey. a better
  • Of all the months, August is ___ (busy) month for Turkish tourism. the busiest
Teacher note — Do items 1–3 together as a class (write on the board), then let students try 4–6 in pairs before revealing. Watch for "more hotter" and "the most best" double-comparative errors — very common at this level.
04

Speaking: Which Holiday Wins?

10 min

In groups of 2–3, students rank the categories below for South Africa vs. Turkey as a summer holiday, then defend their ranking using comparative and superlative sentences.

Beaches
Food
Nightlife
Safety
Value for money
Nature & wildlife
Sentence starters: "I think ___ is more ___ than ___ because..."  ·  "In my opinion, ___ has the most/best ___..."  ·  "For me, the biggest difference is..."

Bring the class back together: each group shares one comparative and one superlative sentence about their ranking.

Teacher note — Push stronger students for opinion language ("I'd argue that...", "Honestly, I think..."). For a quieter group, assign each pair one category instead of all six.
05

Wrap-Up: Exit Ticket

3 min

Go around the group — each student says one superlative sentence about their own summer, real or imaginary.

Example: "The best part of my holiday was the food in Bodrum."

Close by naming the two structures one more time: comparative (-er / more... than) for two things, superlative (the -est / the most) for three or more.

TR Kısa özet: iki şeyi karşılaştırırken "daha", en üstünü söylerken "en" kullanılır.
Teacher note — If time allows, preview next lesson: superlatives with "one of the..." (e.g., "one of the most beautiful beaches"), which this group will need for more natural business travel talk.
